Executive summary

The project in three points.

Screen inventory · heuristic review · journey mapping · interactive prototype

01

Problem

Booking, appointments, queue status and records were separate journeys with inconsistent states and repeated decisions.

02

Approach

Use the appointment as the connecting object and design every screen around the patient's next step.

03

Design outcome

A fifteen-screen prototype connecting doctor discovery, appointment time, confirmation, arrival, queue and follow-up.

Key product decisions

The decisions that changed the experience.

01

Lead with the next appointment

Before

Six equal home actions pushed the patient's most relevant information below the fold.

Design response

Prioritise search, the next appointment, records and contextual follow-up actions.

02

Show real appointment choices

Before

Doctor profiles showed clinic opening hours instead of a time the patient could book.

Design response

Use clear appointment slots and keep the chosen doctor, hospital and time visible.

03

Carry context through the visit

Before

Confirmation, queue status and arrival repeated information or sent patients into separate flows.

Design response

Connect confirmation, queue, room, contact and notifications to the same appointment.

04

Make records useful after care

Before

Empty record areas did not explain what was missing or what the patient should do next.

Design response

Use a chronological record with results, visits, notes and relevant follow-up actions.
